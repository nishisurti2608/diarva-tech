## Audience and list preparation

Choose one initial segment and one relevant service. Two candidates are restaurants needing an ordering experience, or small service businesses needing a better website. Do not pitch the whole service catalogue in the first message.

For each contact, record the business, role, email, source URL/date, specific relevance, and the basis that permits the commercial message. Canada's CASL generally requires consent, sender identification and an unsubscribe mechanism. Publication of an email address is not blanket permission; applicability depends on the circumstances. B2B exceptions also have conditions. Keep evidence and a suppression list. Source: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm

Do not use a purchased/scraped list on the assumption that every address is eligible. Before sending, verify the actual recipients and consent basis, final copy, sender account, and sending scope with the owner.

## Sending preparation

- Make the public demo/website accessible to the intended recipients before linking to it. A successful HTTP response alone does not prove they can view a private Site.
- Verify SPF, DKIM and DMARC with the mailbox provider for diarva.org. No DNS or mailbox settings have been changed in this task.
- Keep the first campaign narrow and personalized; do not use fake replies, fake prior relationships, fabricated results, or the sample testimonials as evidence.
- Process opt-outs and suppress future marketing messages. Configure a monitored reply mailbox; reply-based unsubscribe must be operational, not just wording in a draft.
- No tracking pixels are included in these drafts. If campaign links are used, use campaign codes only, such as utm_source=outreach&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=business_websites; never include recipient names or email addresses in URLs.
- Measure relevant replies, calls and confirmed enquiries rather than treating opens or clicks as sales.
